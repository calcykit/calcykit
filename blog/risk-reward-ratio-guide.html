Most beginner traders obsess over one question — what percentage of their trades are winners. But professional traders know that win rate alone means almost nothing. A trader winning 70% of trades can still lose money consistently. A trader winning only 35% can be highly profitable. The difference is risk reward ratio — the single most important metric in trading.

What is Risk Reward Ratio?

Risk reward ratio compares how much you could lose on a trade to how much you could gain. It is expressed as 1:X where X is the multiple of your risk you stand to earn.

  • 1:1 ratio — you risk $100 to potentially make $100
  • 1:2 ratio — you risk $100 to potentially make $200
  • 1:3 ratio — you risk $100 to potentially make $300

The ratio is defined by three price levels on every trade — your entry price, your stop loss (maximum loss point), and your take profit (target exit point). These three levels must be decided before entering any trade.

The fundamental rule: Never enter a trade without knowing your stop loss and take profit levels first. If you cannot define them before entering, you have no business being in the trade. R:R ratio only exists when these levels are set in advance.

How to Calculate Risk Reward Ratio

Risk Reward Ratio Formula
Risk = Entry Price − Stop Loss Price Reward = Take Profit Price − Entry Price R:R Ratio = Reward / Risk

Break-Even Win Rate = Risk / (Risk + Reward) × 100

A ratio of 1:3 means your reward is 3× your risk on this trade

Why Win Rate is Misleading

Here is the uncomfortable truth about win rate that most trading educators never tell you clearly:

TraderWin RateR:R RatioResult After 100 Trades ($100 risk each)
Beginner A70%1:0.5−$2,500 LOSS
Beginner B50%1:1$0 Break Even
Professional C35%1:3+$4,000 PROFIT
Professional D40%1:2+$2,000 PROFIT

Beginner A wins 70% of trades — but because they risk $100 to make only $50 on each trade, 30 losses wipe out all gains from 70 wins and more. Professional C wins only 35% of trades but makes 3× on winners — 35 winning trades at $300 each earn $10,500 while 65 losing trades at $100 each cost only $6,500 — a net profit of $4,000.

The trap most beginners fall into: Cutting winners short to lock in small profits while letting losers run hoping they recover. This produces a high win rate with terrible R:R. The result is consistent losses despite winning more often than losing.

Minimum Win Rate Needed to Be Profitable

For any given R:R ratio there is a minimum win rate below which you lose money regardless of everything else. This is your break-even win rate:

Break-Even Win Rate Formula
Break-Even Win % = 1 / (1 + R:R Ratio) × 100

Win above this rate at your target R:R and you are profitable long-term

The liberating insight: With a 1:3 R:R ratio you can be wrong on 75% of your trades and still break even. Win just 30% and you are profitable. This transforms trading from a pressure-filled guessing game into a mathematical edge you can rely on systematically.

How to Set Stop Loss and Take Profit Properly

Stop Loss Placement

Your stop loss should be placed at a level where your trade idea is definitively wrong — not just temporarily uncomfortable.

  • Place below key support — not at a round number where everyone else puts theirs
  • Use ATR (Average True Range) — stop = 1.5× to 2× ATR below entry prevents noise-triggered exits
  • Never move stop further away to avoid being stopped out — this is how small losses become account-killing losses
  • Set stop first — then calculate position size based on it using our position size calculator

Take Profit Placement

  • Place at the next major resistance level — where sellers are likely to appear
  • Use Fibonacci extensions — the 1.618 and 2.618 levels are common institutional targets
  • Consider partial exits — take 50% profit at 1:2 and let the rest run to 1:3 or beyond
  • Never move take profit lower just to lock in profit early — this kills your R:R ratio

Common Risk Reward Mistakes

1. Entering Without Defined Levels

Entering a trade without a pre-defined stop loss and take profit is not trading — it is gambling. You cannot calculate R:R ratio without both levels clearly defined before entry. No levels, no trade.

2. Accepting Bad R:R Because the Setup Feels Strong

Confidence in a trade has zero mathematical impact on its outcome. A 1:0.8 ratio setup that feels 90% certain is still a negative expectancy trade. Skip it regardless of how good it looks.

3. Moving Stop Loss to Break Even Too Early

Moving your stop to break even the moment a trade moves in your favor seems safe. But doing it too early gets you stopped out on normal price fluctuation before the trade reaches its target. Let trades breathe.

4. Inconsistent Application

R:R ratio only creates an edge when applied consistently across many trades. Taking 1:3 setups on some trades and 1:0.8 setups on others because they feel good destroys the mathematical edge entirely.

5. Ignoring Fees

Exchange fees reduce your effective reward on every winning trade. On a $10,000 position at 0.1% fee each side you pay $20 in fees. Over 100 trades that is $2,000 — which can eliminate the profit from several winning trades entirely.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is risk reward ratio?+

Risk reward ratio compares the potential profit of a trade to its potential loss. A 1:3 ratio means you risk $1 to potentially make $3. Professional traders only take trades with a minimum 1:2 ratio to ensure long-term profitability regardless of win rate.

What is a good risk reward ratio?+

A minimum 1:2 ratio is the professional standard — risk $1 to make $2. Experienced traders target 1:3 or higher. With a 1:2 ratio you can be profitable with only a 34% win rate which makes consistent trading psychologically sustainable over hundreds of trades.

How is risk reward ratio calculated?+

Risk = Entry Price minus Stop Loss. Reward = Take Profit minus Entry Price. R:R Ratio = Reward divided by Risk. A ratio of 3 means your potential reward is 3 times your potential risk. Always calculate this before entering any trade.

Can I be profitable with a low win rate?+

Yes. With a 1:3 risk reward ratio you only need to win 25% of trades to break even. Win just 30% and you are profitable over a large sample of trades. This is exactly why professional traders focus on finding high R:R setups rather than trying to increase win rate artificially.

What is the minimum win rate needed to be profitable?+

Break-even win rate = 1 / (1 + R:R ratio) × 100. At 1:2 ratio you break even at 33% wins. At 1:3 ratio you break even at 25% wins. At 1:4 ratio you break even at 20% wins. Win consistently above these thresholds and you are profitable long-term.
